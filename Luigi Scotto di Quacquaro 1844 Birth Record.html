What's solid: the town (Procida), district (Pozzuoli), province (Napoli), the quarter of residence (Sant'Antonio), the child's name (Luigi), the date (1 September 1844), and the surname you supplied — Scotto di Quacquaro — which fits the visible letterforms well, even where the ink itself has faded past confident independent reading.

What's still soft: the exact hour of birth and declaration, the parents' precise professions, the house/street-level detail beyond the quarter name, and a couple of witness particulars. These are faded-ink limits of the scan, not gaps in the historical record — a sharper photograph of the original register (or the register itself, if the Procida comune archive holds it) could resolve most of them.

Dotted underlines above mark words read with reasonable but not full confidence.

Document type: this is an atto di nascita — a civil birth registration, distinct from a church baptismal record — created under the Stato Civile system used across the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies. The printed boilerplate was filled in by hand for each birth; the right-hand margin (visible on page 1) was reserved for a later cross-reference to the child's baptism, once the parish returned confirmation that the Sacrament had been administered.

Sant'Antonio, Procida

Procida's nine grancìe

The island has long been divided into nine historic quarters — grancìe — each once tied to a parish or monastic estate. Sant'Antonio is one of them.

Sant'AntonioLuigi's quarter, 1844

Centered on the 1606 Chiesa di Sant'Antonio Abate; a residential quarter on the island's eastern side, inland from the port.

Terra Murata

The fortified hilltop old town — Procida's oldest settlement, crowned by the Abbey of San Michele.

Corricella

The pastel-stacked fishing village below Terra Murata — the island's most photographed corner.

Sent'cò

Home to Marina Grande, the island's commercial port — almost certainly where Luigi's family arrived and departed by boat.

Chiaiolella

The southwestern marina, facing the islet of Vivara across a footbridge.

1606

Chiesa di Sant'Antonio Abate

The quarter's namesake church was built in 1606 and still stands at the junction with Via Cavour — the most direct physical link left to the neighborhood Luigi's father called home.

1844

A fishing and shipbuilding island

Mid-19th-century Procida's economy ran on fishing and wooden shipbuilding — the professions recorded (however faded) for Luigi's parents and witnesses likely trace back to one of the two.

2022

Italy's Capital of Culture

Procida — long overshadowed by Capri and Ischia — was named Italy's Capital of Culture in 2022, drawing renewed attention to exactly the kind of quiet, lived-in quarters like Sant'Antonio.

04 — Planning a Visit

If you ever want to stand where he stood

Practical notes for a future trip, current as of 2026.

Getting there

Hydrofoil from Naples' Molo Beverello, ~35–40 min, or a slower ferry from Calata di Massa (~60 min). The Pozzuoli route — fittingly, the very district named in the 1844 record — is the shortest crossing, at roughly 30–35 minutes.

Getting around

Procida is under 4 sq km and walkable end to end. Non-resident vehicles are restricted on the island for most of the year, so plan on foot, bike, or local minibus.

Where to stay

Terra Murata and Corricella are the postcard bases, but staying a short walk from Sant'Antonio itself would put you closest to the ground Luigi's family actually lived on.

For the research

The comune's Stato Civile archive (or the Naples State Archive, Archivio di Stato di Napoli) is the next stop for the original register — worth an email ahead of any trip to arrange access.
